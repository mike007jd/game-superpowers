# Presentation

## Compose for play

Translate the chosen direction into camera, silhouette, palette, materials, lighting and motion at the actual playing scale. Make the subject, useful space and relevant signals readable together. Keep this visual language coherent across the world and interface.

Diagnose weak images by layer: composition, form, material, lighting, effects or overlay. Fix the responsible layer before adding decoration. Preserve gameplay positions and collision during visual changes unless changing them is part of the request.

## Make feedback causal

Bind animation, VFX, camera response and sound to actual game events. Distinguish acknowledgment, success, denial and warning where those states exist. Important consequences should be perceivable with audio off and through more than color alone.

Shape anticipation, action, consequence and recovery around the next decision. Choose buffering, easing, hit-stop, shake or secondary motion only where they support the intended feel. Inspect repeated actions and interruptions: a dramatic peak frame can hide unreadable timing or an obstructed next input.

Give sound a hierarchy so useful cues survive busy moments. Match accents to perceived events, vary repetition where it becomes tiring, and make mute or volume controls respect the player's choice.

## Observe the right medium

Judge composition at the intended viewport and camera distance, motion continuously at ordinary speed, and sound by listening. A source change establishes wiring, not the resulting look or feel. Integrate generated assets into their real scenes and triggers before judging the improvement.
